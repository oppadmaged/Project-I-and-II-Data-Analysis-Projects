# Project 2: Impact of Payment Methods on Daily Spending in Egypt

---

## Description

This project explores the influence of the various methods of payment-Cash, Debit/Credit Cards, and E- Wallets-on daily non-essential consumer expenditure and impulsive purchase behavior among consumers in Egypt, within the 18-24 age group.

---

## Research Question

> Does the form of payment (cash vs. Digital) effect one's spending patterns throughout the day and purchase frequency of 'impulsive' items?

---

## Hypothesis

Those using digital payment forms will be observed to spend their money more carelessly, and make more frequent 'emergent' (unplanned) purchases than those using physical cash.

---

## Data Set Information

| Field | Details |
|---|---|
| **Sample Size** | 51 respondents |
| **Population** | Age 18-24 in Egypt |
| **Collection Method** | Online Survey hosted on Google Forms, convenience sampling |
| **Variables** | Age, Gender, Main payment form, Daily non-essential spend (EGP), 'emergent' purchase frequency, and number of subscriptions |

---

## Files in this Repository

1. **`project2.csv`** — Raw data from the survey responses, contains 51 respondents with 11 feature columns.
2. **`ProjectIIAnalysis.ipynb`** — A Jupyter Notebook that contains all the data cleaning (extraction of the midpoint from range values) and analysis (descriptive stats & data visualization-bar charts, pie charts, and box plots) done to produce the results.
3. **`Project2-Report.pdf`** — The full project report which contains an introduction to the problem, the method used for analysis, the statistical data found, and the conclusion.

---

## Main Findings

- **Average Spending:** Users who predominantly use Debit/Credit cards have the highest spending (138.21 EGP), those who use cash spend the least (111.08 EGP), and those who use E-Wallets are somewhere in the middle (85.10 EGP).
- **Digital Users:** 51% of the surveyed sample base prefer digital forms of payment to physical cash.
- **Conclusion:** Physical payment is no longer used by the majority, digital payment methods (cards) lead to a lesser feeling of spending money, and result in consumers spending more.
